### PT-5, the 29 October session

Counted as a participant, decided 1 Nov, reversing the 31 October call that had kept it out as a
pilot. It is numbered last because it ran first, on an earlier prototype and the first version of
the task sheet.

Two things about it have to stay visible in the report, because a reader will notice them:

- **It is a partial run.** 3 minutes 40 seconds against a protocol budgeting about 25 minutes for
  five tasks. It reached T1 and T2, with T2 not completed.
- **It used a laptop, not a phone.** The transcript opens with the participant describing working
  "on the desktop on the fake prototype". A laptop participant cannot react to the one-handed,
  outdoors reality the design assumes.

It earned its place regardless: it surfaced the "station" word collision, which was fixed before
anyone else sat down.

The laptop point is not unique to PT-5, and it is worse than the earlier draft assumed. With the
device column now complete, PT-1, PT-3 and PT-5 ran on laptops and only PT-2 and PT-4 on phones:
**three of the five think-aloud sessions ran on a laptop**, a majority of the observation half.
The design assumes one-handed outdoor use at a landmark, so the report should carry this as a
count and a limitation rather than treating PT-5 as the lone exception.

**Still needed from the team:** the notes themselves, and a date for each session.

### The caveat on PQ-1 and PQ-2

Both are counted, decided 1 Nov. What has to travel with them is how they were collected: a team
member entered each from a spoken debrief rather than the respondent completing the form. Their
open answers carry quoted speech throughout: "no context for any background. I was asked to do
something.", "the yellow thing", "I do not like people". The report already observed that they
"repeat wording taken from the think-aloud debriefs".

Two consequences for the write-up:

- **They are transcribed, not self-completed.** A questionnaire mediated by a team member is a
  different instrument from one filled in alone, and the moderator's phrasing can shape the
  answers. Say so in the limitations paragraph.
- **They may not be independent people.** If the speakers were PT-1 and PT-2, those two sit in
  both technique groups, which the handout forbids. The distinct-person count is 11 if they are
  other people and 9 if they are not, which is why both figures appear in the headline table.

The PT-1 and PT-2 details recorded on 1 Nov are partly consistent with that reading. PQ-2 is an
18-to-24 scientist and PT-2 is an 18-to-24 science student, and both PQ rows answer No to
INFO90007 and No to prior 3D apps exactly as both PT rows do. Two fields pull the other way:
PQ-1 is a student who also works as a student experience manager, which neither matches nor
contradicts PT-1's commerce and marketing study, and PQ-1 and PQ-2 both say Phone while PT-1 ran
on a laptop.

So the demographics neither confirm nor rule out the overlap. The independence problem stands on
its own evidence, which is the quoted speech in the open answers, and it is disclosed rather than
resolved.

**Needed from the team:** confirmation of whose debriefs these were. If they were two other
people, the overlap problem disappears and only the collection-method caveat remains.

The content does double duty either way: it is usable as think-aloud debrief evidence as well as
questionnaire data, and the open answers are quoted in the findings on that basis.

### The caveat on PQ-7

PQ-7 answered 4 or 5 to every SUS item, agreeing that the system is easy to use (5) **and**
cumbersome (5), that they felt confident (5) **and** needed to learn a lot (5). Every
reverse-scored item agrees with its opposite, which is a straight-line response rather than a
judgement.

The team decided on 1 Nov to include it. That is defensible, but only if the report says the
pattern is there. Note it in the limitations paragraph rather than letting a marker find it in
Appendix C.
