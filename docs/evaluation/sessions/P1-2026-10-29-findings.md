## Findings

### F-a · "Station" collides with a railway station name - **severity 3**

The clearest finding in the session. The participant was asked which **station** they had been
assigned, and answered with a **landmark** instead:

> *"So there are for this street station... [Flinders] street station is where I am"*

They then stalled completely:

> *"I can't continue what I've been to really what is this anyway"*

The prototype uses *station* to mean an assigned position around the landmark, while the mock
session list contains **Flinders Street Station** as a place. One word, two meanings, on adjacent
screens. T2 was not completed.

**Suggested fix:** rename the concept - *your spot*, *your position*, or *your post* - or remove
Flinders Street Station from the mock data. Renaming is the better fix: the collision would recur
with any real station, and plenty of landmarks are stations.

### F-b · Too many controls on the capture screens - **severity 3**

Said four separate times, unprompted, and the participant's closing answer when asked what they
would change:

> *"there's too many buttons"*
> *"the confusing moments were because there was so many buttons and I couldn't find out what's happening"*
> *"the screen overall is too busy... I would make it more simplified"*

**Suggested fix:** reduce the capture and briefing screens to one primary action each and demote
the rest. This is a Nielsen *aesthetic and minimalist design* problem.

### F-c · The live session was not findable under Nearby - **severity 2**

> *"I clicked a community because nearby there wasn't anything"*

T1 asks the participant to join a session happening now. They could not find it where the design
assumes they will look, and reached it through Community instead. Worth checking whether this is a
prototype wiring gap or a genuine discoverability problem.

### F-d · Capture-versus-share confusion on an already-captured landmark - **severity 2**

> *"Request and they share this model. No, that's because it's been done already... that doesn't
> make sense because I need to capture it right"*

The landmark page offers sharing an existing model and requesting a new capture without making
clear which applies. The participant reasoned their way out of it, but out loud and slowly.

## Two results that support existing design decisions

**The quick-callout design is validated.** Asked about the voice channel:

> *"voice channel makes it a lot easier... but in public I think maybe there's a button that I can
> press that will be better"*

A participant independently asking for a tap instead of speaking aloud in public is direct
evidence for the one-tap callouts. Worth quoting in §3.3 - it is exactly the "designing around
stranger awkwardness" argument, now with evidence rather than assertion.

**But the stranger premise got a soft answer.** On willingness to join:

> *"I would join a session if I was interested in capturing photos with... friends"*

Friends, not strangers. One data point, and possibly a transcription slip, but the whole concept
rests on strangers cooperating. Watch whether later participants say the same - if they do, it
belongs in §4 as an honest limitation rather than being quietly left out.
