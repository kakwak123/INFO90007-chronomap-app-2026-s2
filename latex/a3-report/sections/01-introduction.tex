\section{Introduction}

Three-dimensional models of physical places are normally produced with dedicated equipment such as
LiDAR scanners or purpose-built camera rigs, which are expensive and impractical for most people.
Where public 3D data does exist it comes from proprietary platforms that choose which locations to
cover and when to refresh them, and no member of the public can request that a place be captured or
re-captured.

ChronoMap 3D addresses this by treating a group of ordinary phones as one distributed camera.
People who are at the same place at the same time - typically strangers - are organised into a
short, co-located capture window, and their photographs are reconstructed into a single 3D model of
that place. A host reads a live coverage radar and assigns each participant to a side nobody has yet
covered, so the group behaves as a deliberately positioned array rather than a crowd taking
overlapping snapshots.
